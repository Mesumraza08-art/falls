Japan · Destination guide

Discover the Magic of Mount Fuji, Japan’s Iconic Peak.

By the Fan Travel Service team

As a travel agent, I invite you to explore the breathtaking beauty and cultural significance of Mount Fuji, Japan’s most iconic natural landmark. Whether you’re seeking adventure, serenity or a glimpse into rich Japanese heritage, Mount Fuji offers something unforgettable for every traveller.

Mount Fuji is not just a mountain, but a symbol of Japan’s cultural and spiritual legacy, represented throughout the country’s art, literature and religion. As Japan’s highest mountain, it is also recognised as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

A mountain for every season

Mount Fuji is more than a summer destination. In spring the surrounding area is adorned with cherry blossoms, while autumn brings an array of vibrant foliage. Winter offers a beautiful snow-covered landscape, perfect for picture-perfect moments.

Getting there

Unlike many remote mountains, Mount Fuji is easily accessible from major cities like Tokyo and Kyoto. You can go with a tour company, solo or with friends. A train or bus ride brings you to its base, where you can enjoy stunning views from various points, including the famous Five Lakes area.

Mount Fuji is about 100 kilometres (62 miles) southwest of Tokyo. There are several ways to reach it, so plan ahead and keep track of bus and train schedules. Watch the weather too, as conditions can change rapidly. The journey from Tokyo to Kawaguchiko Station typically takes about two to three hours.

  • By train: take the JR Chuo Line from Tokyo to Otsuki Station, then transfer to the Fujikyu Railway to Kawaguchiko Station.
  • By bus: buses run directly from several locations in Tokyo, including Shinjuku and Tokyo Station, to the Fuji Five Lakes area or the Subaru Fifth Station.

Kawaguchiko to the Yoshida fifth station

The bus from Kawaguchiko Station to the Yoshida fifth station takes about 55 minutes and is the most convenient option. Buy tickets at the bus terminal at Kawaguchiko Station. Buses run more frequently during the climbing season (July to early September), starting from 6:30 am, with the last bus down at 5:30 pm.

At the fifth station

At the Yoshida fifth station you will be at an altitude of 7,562 ft, with breathtaking views all around. Bring a sweater, as it gets cooler at this altitude even in summer.

The fifth station is the starting point for climbers attempting the summit, and hiking here is popular with locals and visitors alike in summer. It marks the halfway point of the Yoshida Trail; other routes include the Subashiri, Gotemba and Fujinomiya trails. The official climbing season runs from early July to early September, and the climb from the fifth station usually takes about five to six hours. At the time of writing, the park fee was ¥2,000 per climber.

Several shops sell Mount Fuji souvenirs, including walking sticks that climbers have stamped at each station on the way to the top, plus postcards, snacks, local crafts and ice cream. You can also find hiking supplies if you have missed any last-minute items for your climb.

A few restaurants offer coffee and snacks, both Japanese and Western. Try Mount Fuji steamed buns while soaking in the views from the outdoor seating areas.

The station is also home to Komitake Shrine, a small Shinto shrine where climbers pray for a safe journey to the summit. Take a few minutes to savour this experience.

A photographer’s paradise

From sweeping views of the surrounding area to close shots of Mount Fuji’s rugged terrain, the fifth station offers plenty of chances to capture stunning images. Try the different lookout areas; the one next to the shrine is a must.
